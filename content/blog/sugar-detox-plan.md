"Sugar detox" sounds like juice cleanses and strict rules. This plan is the opposite. You do not need special products or a perfect week. You take one small, specific step each day, and by day seven you have cut most of your added sugar and know what triggers your cravings.

## Why one step a day works

Big overnight changes fail because they ask for willpower at every meal. Small steps build on each other: once your drink is swapped (day 2) and your breakfast has protein (day 3), the 4 pm crash gets smaller, so the cravings you plan for on day 4 are easier to handle.

## The plan, day by day

**Day 1: find your sources.** Read the labels of five things you eat often and look at the *Added Sugars* line. Divide by 4 to get sugar cubes. Our [daily sugar calculator](/blog/how-much-sugar-per-day/) does the maths for a whole day.

**Day 2: swap your drink.** Sweet drinks are usually the biggest single source. Replace one with water, sparkling water or unsweetened tea. A can of soda alone has about [10 sugar cubes](/blog/how-much-sugar-in-coke/).

**Day 3: protein breakfast.** Eggs, Greek yogurt, beans, nuts or leftovers. A breakfast with protein and fibre keeps you full longer than cereal or pastries.

**Day 4: plan the danger zone.** Most people have one or two craving times a day. Write an if-then plan: "If it is 3 pm and I want something sweet, then I will walk around the block first."

**Day 5: clear the reach.** Put sweets out of sight or out of the house. Put fruit, nuts and tea where the sweets used to be.

**Day 6: ride one wave.** When a craving hits, do not fight it. Breathe slowly for two minutes or walk for fifteen. Cravings rise, peak and fade. Here is more on [how to stop sugar cravings](/blog/how-to-stop-sugar-cravings/).

**Day 7: notice the change.** Eat a piece of fruit slowly. Many people find it tastes sweeter than a week ago. Write down what feels different.

## What to expect

The first three to five days are often the hardest. Some people report headaches, tiredness, irritability or strong cravings. Research on this is still early, but these feelings usually ease within a couple of weeks. Read our guide to [sugar withdrawal symptoms and timeline](/blog/sugar-withdrawal-symptoms-timeline/) for details.

## What to eat during the week

- **Meals:** vegetables, a protein (eggs, fish, chicken, tofu, beans) and some whole grains or potatoes.
- **Snacks:** fruit, nuts, plain yogurt with berries, hummus with vegetables, cheese. More ideas in [what to eat when you crave sugar](/blog/what-to-eat-when-craving-sugar/).
- **Drinks:** water, sparkling water, coffee and tea without sugar.

## If you slip

One cookie does not undo the week. Ask what led to it (tired, stressed, a party) and carry on with the next step. A slip is information, not failure.
