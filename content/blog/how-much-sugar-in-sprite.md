Sprite has a gentle image. It is clear, it has no caffeine, it is what people reach for when their stomach is upset, and it often gets ordered as the "lighter" choice next to cola. So it surprises a lot of people that, sugar-wise, it is basically the same drink as Coke.

The short answer: **a 12 oz can of Sprite has 38 grams of sugar.** That is about 9 to 10 sugar cubes, and more than a full day's suggested added sugar for most women.

## How much sugar is in Sprite? The numbers by size

The US label lists 38 g of sugar in a 12 fl oz (355 ml) can. Other sizes scale from that, so the numbers below are close estimates. Check your own bottle, because sizes and recipes vary.

| Size | Sugar | Cubes (about 4 g each) |
|---|---|---|
| 7.5 oz mini can (222 ml) | ~24 g | ~6 |
| 12 oz can (355 ml) | 38 g | ~9 to 10 |
| 16.9 oz bottle (500 ml) | ~54 g | ~13 to 14 |
| 20 oz bottle (591 ml) | ~63 to 65 g | ~16 |
| 2 liter bottle, whole | ~210 g | ~53 |
| Sprite Zero Sugar, any size | 0 g | 0 |

The 20 oz bottle is where most people get caught out. It feels like "one drink", but it holds well over twice what the American Heart Association suggests for a whole day.

## Sprite vs. Coke and other sodas

Clear sodas are not lighter in sugar than dark ones. Per 12 oz can, using typical US labels:

- **Sprite:** 38 g
- **Coca-Cola:** 39 g
- **Mountain Dew:** 46 g

The real differences between Sprite and Coke are caffeine (Sprite has none) and color, not sugar. If you want the full breakdown for cola, see [how much sugar is in Coke](/blog/how-much-sugar-in-coke/).

## What kind of sugar is in Sprite?

In the US, Sprite is sweetened with **high fructose corn syrup**. In many other countries it is made with cane or beet sugar, and in some places, such as the UK, the recipe was changed to use less sugar plus low-calorie sweeteners after sugar taxes came in. That is why the same brand can show very different numbers on labels around the world.

For your body, the type matters less than the amount. High fructose corn syrup and table sugar are both roughly half glucose and half fructose, and both count as **added sugar** in the limits health groups use.

## Sprite and your daily sugar limit

The American Heart Association suggests at most about 25 g of added sugar a day for most women and 36 g for most men. The World Health Organization suggests keeping free sugars under 10% of daily calories, ideally closer to 5%.

So:

- **One 12 oz can (38 g)** goes past the suggested daily amount for most women and slightly past it for most men.
- **One 20 oz bottle (~64 g)** is close to double it, even for men.
- **A mini can (~24 g)** fits just under the suggested amount for most women.

If you want to see your own number, use the calculator in [how much sugar per day](/blog/how-much-sugar-per-day/).

One soda now and then is not the issue. It just helps to know that a "light-looking" drink counts the same as any other soda.

## Is Sprite good for an upset stomach?

Many people grew up with flat Sprite or ginger ale when they were sick. Some find a few sips settle nausea, and that is fine. But sodas are not designed for rehydration: they are high in sugar and low in salt, and a lot of sugar at once can make diarrhea worse for some people. For real fluid loss, an oral rehydration solution from a pharmacy is made for that job.

## Sprite Zero Sugar

Sprite Zero has **0 g of sugar**. It is sweetened with aspartame and acesulfame potassium. Because it contains aspartame, it is not suitable for people with phenylketonuria (PKU), and the can says so.

For many people, a zero-sugar version is a useful bridge. It keeps the habit (the cold can, the fizz, the lemon-lime taste) while removing the sugar. Some people stay there, others use it as a step toward sparkling water. Both are reasonable.

## Easy ways to drink less Sprite

If a can or bottle is part of your day, these tend to work better than trying to quit cold:

- **Go down a size.** A 7.5 oz mini can has about a third less sugar than a regular can and still feels like a treat.
- **Mix it.** Half Sprite, half plain sparkling water. Same taste and fizz, half the sugar. Most people stop noticing within a couple of weeks.
- **Make your own lemon-lime.** Sparkling water with fresh lemon and lime slices, and a few mint leaves if you like.
- **Switch the routine, not just the drink.** If Sprite comes with lunch or the afternoon slump, keep the moment and change what is in the glass.
- **Plan a step-down.** Our [sugar detox plan](/blog/sugar-detox-plan/) shows how to cut back over a few weeks without white-knuckling it.

If your daily drink is something stronger, like an energy drink, the same ideas apply. See [how much sugar is in Red Bull](/blog/how-much-sugar-in-red-bull/).

## When to talk to a doctor

If you have diabetes or prediabetes, talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian about sugary drinks, including how to use them if your care team has told you to treat low blood sugar with juice or soda. Also check in if you are using soda to cope with frequent nausea, vomiting or dehydration, since those can need medical care.

## Summary

- A 12 oz can of Sprite has 38 g of sugar, about 9 to 10 cubes.
- A 20 oz bottle has about 63 to 65 g, roughly 16 cubes.
- Sprite has almost the same sugar as Coke; it just has no caffeine.
- Sprite Zero has no sugar and contains aspartame.
- Smaller cans and half-and-half mixes are easy first steps.
