A 12 oz (355 ml) can of Coca-Cola has **39 grams of sugar**. That is about **10 sugar cubes**, or a little over 9 teaspoons, all of it added sugar. One can is already more than the 25 g a day the American Heart Association suggests for most women, and most of the 36 g it suggests for men ([AHA](https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/healthy-eating/eat-smart/sugar/added-sugars)).

## See it in sugar cubes

Pick a drink. Each cube is about 4 g of sugar. Red cubes go past a 25 g daily limit.

## Coke sizes compared

| Size | Sugar | Sugar cubes |
|---|---|---|
| 7.5 oz mini can | 25 g | about 6 |
| 12 oz can | 39 g | about 10 |
| 16.9 oz bottle | 55 g | about 14 |
| 20 oz bottle | 65 g | about 16 |
| 2 liter bottle | about 216 g | about 54 |

Values are from US Coca-Cola labels and can change by country and recipe. Many countries outside the US now sell lower-sugar versions, so check the label where you live.

## Why a drink hits differently from food

Sugar in a drink goes down fast and does not fill you up the way food does. You can finish 39 g in a few minutes and still feel just as hungry at your next meal. That is why sweet drinks are usually the easiest and biggest win when you cut back.

## Easy swaps that still feel like a treat

- **Sparkling water** with lime, lemon or a splash of juice.
- **Coke Zero Sugar or Diet Coke** as a bridge. They have no sugar, and many people use them for a few weeks while their taste for sweetness resets.
- **Unsweetened iced tea** with mint or lemon.
- **Half and half.** If you cannot quit cold, mix half soda and half sparkling water for a week, then go further.
