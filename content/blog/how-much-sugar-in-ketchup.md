Ketchup is rarely the first thing people think of when they start cutting back on sugar. It sits next to fries and burgers, and it is the fries and the soda that get the attention. But ketchup is one of the sweetest everyday sauces, and it is often used in bigger amounts than people think.

The short answer: **regular ketchup has about 4 grams of sugar per tablespoon, roughly one sugar cube, and most of it is added sugar.** On its own that is small. Where it adds up is in a generous squeeze, a pile of packets, or a sauce-heavy meal with ketchup plus BBQ sauce plus a sweet dressing.

## How much sugar is in ketchup?

A US nutrition label for regular tomato ketchup usually lists a serving as 1 tablespoon (17 g), with about 4 g of total sugars and about 4 g of added sugars. That makes ketchup roughly 20 to 25 percent sugar by weight.

| Amount | Sugar | Cubes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 packet (~9 g) | ~2 g | ~0.5 |
| 1 tablespoon (17 g) | ~4 g | ~1 |
| 3 tablespoons (a burger and fries) | ~12 g | ~3 |
| 100 g | ~22 g | ~5 |
| Whole 20 oz bottle | ~150 g | ~37 |

One sugar cube is about 4 grams. Brands differ, so your bottle may be a little higher or lower.

## Why is there sugar in ketchup?

Tomatoes are naturally a little sweet, but most of the sugar in ketchup is added. It balances the vinegar and gives ketchup its familiar sweet and sour taste. In the US it often comes from high fructose corn syrup or sugar; in other countries it is usually sugar or glucose syrup. Either way, the body handles it as added sugar.

Only a small part comes from the tomatoes themselves. You can see this in "no sugar added" ketchups, which usually show 0 to 1 gram of sugar per tablespoon: that is roughly what the tomato brings.

## Ketchup vs. other condiments

Ketchup is not the sweetest sauce on the shelf. Typical values per serving, from US labels:

- **BBQ sauce (2 tbsp):** often 12 to 17 g. Sweet, smoky styles are among the most sugary sauces.
- **Sweet chili sauce (2 tbsp):** often around 10 to 14 g.
- **Teriyaki sauce (1 tbsp):** often 2 to 5 g, more in thick glazes.
- **Honey mustard (2 tbsp):** often 5 to 8 g.
- **Ketchup (1 tbsp):** about 4 g.
- **Sriracha (1 tsp):** about 1 g.
- **Yellow mustard, hot sauce, mayonnaise, soy sauce:** usually 0 to 1 g.

Note the serving sizes. BBQ and sweet chili labels use 2 tablespoons, ketchup uses 1, so compare per 100 grams if you want a fair fight. Bottled salad dressings, pasta sauces and marinades can carry hidden sugar too; [added sugar vs. natural sugar](/blog/added-sugar-vs-natural-sugar/) explains how to spot it on the label.

## Does ketchup sugar matter?

It depends on how much you use. The American Heart Association suggests keeping added sugar to about 25 grams a day for most women and 36 grams for most men. The World Health Organization recommends keeping free sugars under 10 percent of daily energy, and ideally under 5 percent.

A tablespoon of ketchup is about 4 g, a small slice of that. But a meal with three tablespoons of ketchup and a couple of spoons of BBQ sauce can reach 20 g or more, close to a full day's suggested limit for some people, before any drink or dessert. If you want to see where your day lands, our guide to [how much sugar per day](/blog/how-much-sugar-per-day/) has a quick calculator.

The point is not to fear ketchup. It is that condiments are an easy, painless place to trim sugar once you know where it hides.

## How to find a lower sugar ketchup

1. **Read the "Added Sugars" line.** Regular ketchup reads about 4 g per tablespoon. "No sugar added" and "reduced sugar" versions read 0 to 2 g.
2. **Check the sweetener.** Many no sugar added ketchups use sucralose or stevia. That is a matter of taste and preference; if you would rather avoid sweeteners, look for ketchups sweetened only with tomato, or just use less of the regular kind.
3. **Compare the serving size.** A label with 1 teaspoon servings looks lower but is not.
4. **Watch "organic" and "natural."** These ketchups are often sweetened with cane sugar or honey and can have as much sugar as regular ketchup.

## 6 easy ways to use less sugary sauce

1. **Dip, don't pour.** Putting ketchup in a small dish instead of squeezing it over fries usually means using less.
2. **Mix it half and half.** Ketchup stirred with plain yogurt, mustard or a little hot sauce keeps the taste with less sugar per bite.
3. **Lean on savory flavor.** Mustard, salsa, pickles, garlic sauce and hot sauce are low in sugar and full of taste.
4. **Make a quick ketchup.** Tomato paste, vinegar, a pinch of salt, onion powder and a small amount of sweetener of your choice takes two minutes.
5. **Pick one sweet sauce per meal.** Ketchup or BBQ sauce, not both.
6. **Let your taste adjust.** Like with coffee or tea, sauces that seemed bland at first often taste right after a couple of weeks.

## When to talk to a doctor

If you have diabetes, prediabetes or another condition where you track carbohydrates, ask your doctor or a dietitian how sauces and condiments fit your plan. The same goes if you are thinking about large diet changes for a child. This article is general information, not medical advice.

## The short version

- Ketchup has about 4 g of sugar per tablespoon, roughly one cube, and most of it is added.
- A packet has about 2 g; a sauce-heavy meal can reach 12 to 20 g.
- BBQ and sweet chili sauce usually have more sugar than ketchup.
- Read the "Added Sugars" line, dip instead of pour, and mix in savory sauces.
