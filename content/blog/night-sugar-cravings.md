If you can go all day without sweets and then lose the battle at 9 pm, you are not alone. Night is when sugar cravings are strongest for many people, and it is not about weak willpower. Several triggers pile up at once.

## Why cravings get worse at night

**You are tired.** Willpower and planning are harder when you are worn out. Poor sleep also makes high-calorie food more appealing: in a UC Berkeley study, sleep-deprived people wanted more high-calorie foods than after a full night ([Greer, Goldstein and Walker, 2013](https://news.berkeley.edu/2013/08/06/poor-sleep-junk-food/)).

**The day's stress has built up.** Sweet food is quick comfort, and evening is when you finally have time to feel the day.

**Habit.** If dessert has followed dinner for years, your brain expects it at the same time every night, hungry or not.

**Boredom and screens.** Sitting in front of a show is a classic snacking cue.

**You did not eat enough earlier.** Skipped lunch or a light dinner can show up as a sugar craving later.

## An evening routine that helps

1. **Eat a proper dinner** with protein, vegetables and some carbs. A too-small dinner sets up a 9 pm raid.
2. **Close the kitchen.** Make a cup of herbal tea after dinner and treat it as the end of eating for the day.
3. **Brush your teeth early.** Almost nothing tastes good right after mint, and it signals "done".
4. **Move the cue.** If you always snack on the couch, sit somewhere else for a week, or keep your hands busy.
5. **Keep sweets out of the house** for the first few weeks. At night, you will not drive to the store for cookies, but you will open the cupboard.
6. **Plan a small swap** for nights you still want something: a few frozen grapes, a square of dark chocolate, yogurt with berries. More ideas in [what to eat when you crave sugar](/blog/what-to-eat-when-craving-sugar/).
7. **Go to bed earlier.** Sometimes the craving is just tiredness asking for energy.

## When the craving hits anyway

Give it ten minutes before deciding. Cravings rise, peak and fade, usually within minutes. Breathe in for four seconds and out for six, and notice where you feel the urge. You can try a 30-second version [on our home page](/#try). Our guide on [how to stop sugar cravings](/blog/how-to-stop-sugar-cravings/) has more in-the-moment tools.

## Track your pattern

Write down when the craving came, how tired you were and what you were doing. After a week you will usually see a clear pattern. [Pipgrove](/) does this with a 30-second evening check-in, and Pro can send a gentle heads-up before your usual craving time.

If night eating feels out of control or comes with guilt and secrecy, please talk to a doctor or an eating disorder helpline. Our [health note](/health.html) lists support lines.
