How Much Sugar Is in Beer? Regular, Light, Non-Alcoholic and Hard Seltzer

Does beer have sugar? How much sugar is in regular, light and non-alcoholic beer, why beer still has carbs, and how hard cider, hard lemonade and seltzer compare, in grams and sugar cubes.

By the Pipgrove team. November 26, 2026, 5 min read.

When you start cutting back on sugar, you begin to look at every drink differently. Beer is a common question, partly because it is made from grain and partly because people know it has "carbs."

The short answer: a 12 oz can of regular or light beer usually has 0 to 1 gram of sugar. Beer is not a sugary drink in the way soda or juice is. What beer does have is carbohydrates and alcohol, and the drinks that sit next to beer in the fridge, like hard cider and hard lemonade, can be very sweet.

How much sugar is in beer? The numbers

Here are typical values for a 12 oz (355 ml) serving.

Drink Typical sugar Cubes
Regular beer (lager, pilsner) ~0 g 0
Light beer under 1 g 0
Non-alcoholic beer ~3 to 10 g ~1 to 2
Hard seltzer ~0 to 2 g 0
Hard cider ~15 to 25 g ~4 to 6
Hard lemonade or other flavored malt drink ~30 g or more ~7 or more
For comparison: Coca-Cola 39 g ~10

One sugar cube is about 4 grams. The beer values come from USDA FoodData Central, which lists regular beer at about 0 g of sugar and light beer at about 0.3 g per 12 oz. The other rows are typical label values and change from brand to brand.

Why beer has carbs but almost no sugar

Beer starts as malted grain soaked in hot water. That turns the grain's starch into a sweet liquid called wort, which is full of maltose and other sugars. Then yeast goes to work and ferments most of those sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide.

What the yeast cannot eat are longer chains called dextrins. They count as carbohydrates on a label, but not as sugar, and they do not taste sweet. That is why a regular beer can have around 13 grams of carbs per can and still list close to 0 grams of sugar. Light beers are brewed so that even more of the carbs get fermented, which leaves around 3 to 6 grams of carbs per can.

So two things are true at once:

  • Beer is low in sugar.
  • Beer is not low in calories. A regular beer has around 150 calories per can and a light beer around 100, and most of that comes from alcohol, which has 7 calories per gram.

Which beer-aisle drinks are the sweet ones?

The surprise for many people is not the beer. It is everything around it.

  • Non-alcoholic beer. Some are made by stopping fermentation early, so more of the malt sugar stays in. Others remove the alcohol after brewing and add little back. That is why the range is so wide. If you drink a lot of them, the brand's nutrition facts are worth a look.
  • Fruit beers, shandies and radlers. These are mixed with juice or lemonade, so they can land anywhere from a few grams to 20 or more.
  • Hard cider. It is made from apple juice, and many popular ciders are sweetened after fermentation. Around 15 to 25 grams per can is common.
  • Hard lemonade, hard tea and similar flavored malt drinks. These are often the sweetest drinks in the cooler, often 30 grams or more per bottle, close to a can of soda.
  • Hard seltzer. Usually 0 to 2 grams of sugar. The sweet taste often comes from flavorings or a little added sugar.

Does sugar in beer count toward your daily limit?

The American Heart Association suggests keeping added sugar to about 25 grams a day for most women and 36 grams for most men. Plain beer adds close to nothing to that number. A hard lemonade or a sweet cider can use most of a 25 gram day in one bottle.

The real link between beer and sugar

Even though beer has little sugar, it can still play a part in a sugar habit:

  • Lower guard. After a couple of drinks, the late-night ice cream or the gas station candy feels a lot easier to say yes to.
  • Salty and sweet snacks together. Beer often comes with chips, wings with sweet sauces, or pretzels with dips. If ketchup or barbecue sauce is a regular part of that, how much sugar is in ketchup shows how quickly sauces add up.
  • Next-day cravings. Alcohol can make sleep lighter later in the night, and a short night is one of the most common reasons people reach for sweets the next afternoon. We cover that pattern in night sugar cravings.

None of this makes beer a "healthy" choice. The World Health Organization has said that no level of alcohol is safe for health. But if your goal is less sugar, plain beer is not where most of the sugar is hiding.

Simple lower-sugar choices in the beer aisle

  1. Plain beer or light beer over cider and hard lemonade if sugar is what you are watching.
  2. Check non-alcoholic beer labels. Some are close to zero, others are not.
  3. Use soda water, not lemon-lime soda, if you make shandies or spritzers at home.
  4. Alternate with water so thirst does not do the drinking.
  5. Decide on the snack before the evening starts, not after the second drink.

When to talk to a doctor

If you have diabetes, especially if you use insulin or other medicines that can cause low blood sugar, talk to your doctor before drinking. Alcohol can lower blood sugar for hours afterward, even when the drink itself has almost no sugar. Also talk to a doctor if you are pregnant, take medicines that interact with alcohol, or find it hard to cut back. This article is general information, not medical advice.

  • Non-alcoholic beer often has a few grams more.
  • Hard cider and hard lemonade are the sweet ones, often 15 to 30 grams or more.

Quick answers

Does beer have sugar?

Very little. In most regular and light beers, yeast ferments nearly all of the sugar from the malt into alcohol, so a 12 oz can usually has 0 to 1 gram of sugar. Beer still has carbohydrates, but they are mostly starch-like dextrins, not sugar.

How much sugar is in a can of beer?

USDA FoodData Central lists regular beer at about 0 grams of sugar per 12 oz can and light beer at well under 1 gram. Sweetened styles, fruit beers and non-alcoholic beers can have more, so check the brand if it publishes nutrition facts.

Does non-alcoholic beer have more sugar than regular beer?

Often, yes. Because many non-alcoholic beers are fermented less or not at all, more malt sugar stays in the drink. Many have around 3 to 10 grams of sugar per 12 oz, and some have more. Brands vary a lot.

Which has more sugar, beer or hard cider?

Hard cider, by a wide margin. A typical 12 oz hard cider has around 15 to 25 grams of sugar, and sweet flavored malt drinks such as hard lemonade can have 30 grams or more. Plain beer is usually close to zero.
