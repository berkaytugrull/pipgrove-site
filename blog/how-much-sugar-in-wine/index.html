How Much Sugar Is in Wine? Red, White, Sparkling and Sweet Wines Compared

How much sugar is in a glass of wine, from dry red and white to prosecco, moscato and port, how to read brut and extra dry labels, and why most of the calories in wine come from alcohol, not sugar.

By the Pipgrove team. November 25, 2026, 5 min read.

If you are cutting back on sugar, wine is one of those drinks that is hard to read. There is no nutrition label on most bottles in the US, the words on the label are in French or Italian, and "dry" and "extra dry" do not mean what you would guess.

The short answer: a 5 oz glass of dry red or dry white wine has about 1 gram of sugar, often less. Sweet wines like moscato and port can have 10 grams or more. In dry wine, the sugar is small. What matters more for most people is the alcohol, and what you eat and drink alongside it.

How much sugar is in wine? The numbers

Wine starts as grape juice, which is very sweet. During fermentation, yeast eats the grape sugar and turns it into alcohol. Whatever sugar the yeast does not finish is called residual sugar, and that is the sugar left in your glass.

Here are typical values per glass. A standard US glass is 5 oz (148 ml); dessert wines are usually poured smaller.

Wine Typical sugar per glass Cubes
Dry red (cabernet, merlot, pinot noir) under 1 g ~0
Dry white (sauvignon blanc, pinot grigio) ~1 g ~0
Brut champagne or cava under 2 g ~0.5
Extra dry prosecco ~2 to 2.5 g ~0.5
Off-dry riesling or rosé ~3 to 6 g ~1
Moscato ~10 to 15 g ~3
Port, 3 oz pour ~7 to 10 g ~2
For comparison: Coca-Cola, 12 oz can 39 g ~10

One sugar cube is about 4 grams. The dry wine values come from USDA FoodData Central, which lists red table wine at about 0.6 g of sugar per 100 g and white table wine at about 1 g. Sweet wines vary a lot from bottle to bottle, so treat those rows as rough ranges.

How to tell how sweet a wine is from the label

US wine bottles rarely list sugar. But there are a few clues.

Sparkling wine words

Sparkling wine is the one place where the label tells you a lot, because the sweetness terms are defined in EU wine rules and widely used on bottles sold in the US. Per liter:

  • Brut nature: under 3 g
  • Extra brut: 0 to 6 g
  • Brut: under 12 g
  • Extra dry: 12 to 17 g
  • Dry (sec): 17 to 32 g
  • Demi-sec: 32 to 50 g
  • Doux: over 50 g

The confusing part is that "extra dry" and "dry" are sweeter than "brut." A lot of popular prosecco is labeled extra dry. Per 5 oz glass, it is still only around 2 grams of sugar, but if you want the least sugar, look for brut or extra brut.

Still wine clues

  • Grape and style. Most red wines are fermented fully dry. Riesling, chenin blanc, gewürztraminer and many rosés range from bone dry to quite sweet.
  • Words like "late harvest," "ice wine," "dessert wine," "moscato" or "sweet red." These are made to be sweet.
  • Alcohol percentage. It is a rough hint, not a rule. A wine at 13 to 15% alcohol has usually had most of its sugar fermented away; a moscato at 5 to 7% alcohol usually has a lot left.
  • The winery's tech sheet. Many wineries publish a "technical sheet" online with residual sugar in grams per liter. Divide by about 7 to get grams per 5 oz glass.

If you buy wine in the EU, bottles made from December 2023 onward must give a nutrition declaration, though it can sit behind a QR code on the label.

Sugar is not the main story in wine

For dry wine, the sugar is almost a rounding error. A glass of dry red has about 120 to 125 calories, and nearly all of that comes from alcohol, which has 7 calories per gram, compared with 4 for sugar. Choosing a drier wine trims a few grams of sugar. Pouring a smaller glass or having one glass instead of two changes a lot more.

There are also a few indirect ways wine and sugar connect:

  • Alcohol lowers your guard. Many people find that the dessert, the late-night snack or the second slice of cake happens more easily after a drink or two.
  • Mixers and cocktails. Sangria, wine spritzers made with soda and frozen wine drinks can carry far more sugar than the wine itself.
  • Sleep. Alcohol can make sleep lighter later in the night, and poor sleep is a common trigger for next-day sweet cravings, which we cover in why do I crave sugar.

The World Health Organization has said that no level of alcohol is safe for health, so wine is not a "healthy swap" for anything. If you drink, the low-sugar choice is simply a dry wine in a modest pour.

Does sugar in wine count toward your daily limit?

The American Heart Association suggests keeping added sugar to about 25 g a day for most women and 36 g for most men. Residual sugar in dry wine is grape sugar left over from fermentation, and at under 1 to 2 grams per glass it barely registers. Sweet wines and sparkling wines with a sweet "dosage" are a different story; a big glass of moscato can be close to half of a 25 g day.

Lower sugar wine habits that are easy to keep

  1. Pick brut or extra brut for bubbles, not extra dry or demi-sec.
  2. Choose dry reds and crisp dry whites as your default still wines.
  3. Pour 5 oz, not a "restaurant" 8 oz. A measured glass makes the biggest difference to both sugar and calories.
  4. Alternate with sparkling water. It slows the pace and helps with thirst.
  5. Skip the sweet mixers. If you like spritzers, use soda water instead of lemon-lime soda.
  6. Plan the after-drinks snack. Decide before the evening starts, so the cookie tin does not decide for you.

When to talk to a doctor

If you have diabetes, especially if you use insulin or medicines that can cause low blood sugar, talk to your doctor before drinking. Alcohol can lower blood sugar for hours afterward, even when the wine itself contains little sugar. Also talk to a doctor if you are pregnant, take medicines that interact with alcohol, or find it hard to cut back on drinking. This article is general information, not medical advice.

The short version

  • Dry red and dry white wine: about 1 gram of sugar per 5 oz glass, often less.
  • On sparkling labels, brut is drier than extra dry.
  • Moscato, late harvest and port are the sweet ones: roughly 7 to 15 grams per pour.
  • In dry wine, the calories come mostly from alcohol, so the pour size matters more than the sugar.

Quick answers

How much sugar is in a glass of wine?

A 5 oz (148 ml) glass of dry red or dry white wine usually has about 1 gram of sugar, often less. Off-dry wines can have around 3 to 6 grams per glass, and sweet styles such as moscato often have 10 grams or more.

Which wine has the least sugar?

Dry still wines, red or white, and sparkling wines labeled brut nature, extra brut or brut. Dry red wines such as cabernet sauvignon, merlot and pinot noir are usually close to zero. Brut sparkling wine can have up to 12 grams per liter, which is under 2 grams per glass.

Does extra dry prosecco have more sugar than brut?

Yes. On sparkling wine labels, extra dry is sweeter than brut. Extra dry means 12 to 17 grams of sugar per liter, while brut means under 12. Per 5 oz glass, that is roughly 2 to 2.5 grams versus under 2.

Is the sugar in wine the main source of its calories?

Not in dry wine. A glass of dry wine has about 120 to 125 calories, and almost all of them come from alcohol, which has 7 calories per gram. The sugar adds only a few calories. In sweet and dessert wines, sugar adds more.
