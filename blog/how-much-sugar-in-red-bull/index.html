How Much Sugar Is in Red Bull? Every Can Size in Sugar Cubes

An 8.4 oz can of Red Bull has 27 g of sugar, about 7 sugar cubes. See every can size, how it compares with soda, and what the caffeine adds.

By the Pipgrove team. November 17, 2026, 5 min read.

An original 8.4 oz (250 ml) can of Red Bull has 27 grams of sugar. That is about 7 sugar cubes, all of it added sugar. One small can already uses up more than the 25 g of added sugar a day the American Heart Association suggests for most women, and most of the 36 g it suggests for most men (AHA).

The bigger cans scale up fast. Here is what each size looks like, why an energy drink is an easy place to cut sugar, and how to swap it without losing your afternoon.

Red Bull sugar by can size

Red Bull lists about 11 g of sugar per 100 ml on its labels. That makes the bigger cans easy to estimate:

Can size Sugar Sugar cubes
8.4 oz (250 ml) 27 g about 7
12 oz (355 ml) about 38 to 39 g about 10
16 oz (473 ml) about 52 g about 13
20 oz (591 ml) about 65 g about 16
Sugarfree or Zero, any size 0 g none

The 8.4 oz figure is from the US label. The larger sizes are worked out from the per-100 ml value, so check the can in your hand: recipes and Editions flavors can differ a little by country.

See it in sugar cubes

Pick a drink. Each cube is about 4 g of sugar. Red cubes go past a 25 g daily limit.

Red Bull vs soda: is it worse than a Coke?

Gram for gram, almost the same. A 12 oz can of Coca-Cola has 39 g of sugar, and a 12 oz Red Bull lands within a gram or so of that. The small 8.4 oz Red Bull has less in total only because the can is smaller. If you want the soda side of the story, see how much sugar is in a Coke.

The real difference is the reason you drink it. Soda is usually a treat or a habit with food. Energy drinks tend to be a tool: you reach for one to get through a slump, a shift, a drive or a deadline. That makes them a daily habit for a lot of people, and a daily 16 oz can adds up to about 52 g of added sugar before anything else you eat.

What about the caffeine?

An 8.4 oz Red Bull has 80 mg of caffeine, about the same as a small cup of coffee. A 16 oz can has roughly twice that. The US Food and Drug Administration says up to 400 mg a day is generally not linked with harmful effects for most healthy adults (FDA). That limit does not apply to everyone: people who are pregnant, sensitive to caffeine, on certain medicines, or under 18 should be more careful.

Caffeine also matters for sugar in a quieter way. A late-afternoon can can make it harder to fall asleep, and short or broken sleep is linked with stronger cravings the next day. If evenings are when sweets win, read why sugar cravings hit at night.

Why the energy drink feels like it works

Part of the lift is the caffeine. Part is simply that the drink is cold, sweet and a small ritual: a break from your desk or the road. That is worth knowing, because it means you can keep most of what you like about the habit while dropping the sugar.

The sugar itself is fast. A drink goes down in minutes and does not fill you up the way food does, so you get 27 to 65 g of sugar with no real effect on hunger at your next meal.

Easy swaps that keep the boost

  • Go sugar-free first. Red Bull Sugarfree or Zero keeps the taste and caffeine with 0 g of sugar. Many people use it as a bridge for a few weeks.
  • Size down. If you drink a 16 oz can, switch to the 8.4 oz. That alone cuts about 25 g of sugar a day.
  • Coffee or tea, plain or lightly sweetened. Same caffeine range, and you control the sugar.
  • Cold sparkling water plus a short walk. Sometimes the slump is boredom or a stiff body more than tiredness. Ten minutes outside works more often than you would think.
  • Fix the 3 pm dip at lunch. A lunch with protein and fibre, such as eggs, beans, chicken, fish or yogurt with whole grains and vegetables, tends to make the afternoon slump smaller. Our post on craving sugar after meals has more on this.

You do not have to change everything at once. Pick one swap, keep it for a week, then add the next.

How much sugar are you having in total?

An energy drink is often only part of the day. Use the daily sugar calculator to add up a normal day, including coffee drinks, snacks and breakfast. Seeing the total once is often enough to show where the easy wins are.

When to talk to a doctor

Talk to a doctor if you have diabetes, prediabetes, a heart rhythm problem or high blood pressure, if you are pregnant, or if you notice a racing heart, chest pain, shaking or trouble sleeping after energy drinks. If you feel you cannot get through the day without several cans, that is also worth raising. This post is general information, not medical advice.

The short version

  • An 8.4 oz Red Bull has 27 g of sugar (about 7 cubes). A 12 oz has about 38 to 39 g, and a 16 oz about 52 g.
  • All of it is added sugar, so it counts fully toward the AHA daily limits of about 25 g for women and 36 g for men.
  • Red Bull Sugarfree and Zero have no sugar but still have caffeine.
  • Sizing down or going sugar-free are the two easiest first steps.

Quick answers

How much sugar is in a small can of Red Bull?

The original 8.4 oz (250 ml) can of Red Bull has 27 g of sugar. At about 4 g per cube, that is roughly 7 sugar cubes.

How much sugar is in a 12 oz Red Bull?

Red Bull has about 11 g of sugar per 100 ml, so a 12 oz (355 ml) can has about 38 to 39 g. That is about the same as a 12 oz can of Coca-Cola.

Is the sugar in Red Bull added sugar?

Yes. The sugar in regular Red Bull comes from sucrose and glucose added during making, so all of it counts toward your daily added sugar limit.

Does Red Bull Sugarfree have sugar?

No. Red Bull Sugarfree and Red Bull Zero have 0 g of sugar. They use low-calorie sweeteners instead, but they still contain caffeine.
