Sugar Cravings Before Your Period: Why They Happen and What Helps

Why sugar cravings often rise in the days before and during your period, what is normal, and calm, practical ways to handle them without guilt.

By the Pipgrove team. November 14, 2026, 5 min read.

If you find yourself reaching for cookies, chocolate or candy in the days before your period, you are far from alone. Food cravings, very often for something sweet, are one of the most commonly reported premenstrual symptoms.

This guide explains why sugar cravings tend to rise before and during your period, what is normal, and a few calm ways to handle them. The goal is not to "beat" your body. It is to feel more in control and less at the mercy of the cookie jar.

Is craving sugar before your period normal?

Yes. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists lists food cravings among the common symptoms of premenstrual syndrome (PMS), along with bloating, tiredness, irritability and trouble sleeping. The US Office on Women's Health notes that most people who menstruate have at least one PMS symptom at some point.

Cravings usually show up in the luteal phase, the one to two weeks between ovulation and your period, and they often fade within the first day or two of bleeding.

Why do sugar cravings get stronger before your period?

Researchers are still working out the exact reasons, and it is probably a mix of several things rather than one cause.

  • Hormone shifts. Estrogen and progesterone rise and then fall during the second half of your cycle. These changes can affect appetite and mood.
  • A slightly bigger appetite. Several studies have found that many people eat a bit more in the days before their period. Feeling hungrier often shows up as wanting quick, sweet food.
  • Mood and stress. Irritability, low mood or anxiety before a period are common. Sweet food is a fast, familiar comfort, so the brain asks for it.
  • Tiredness and poor sleep. PMS can disturb sleep, and short sleep on its own tends to raise cravings for sweet, high-calorie foods.
  • Habit. If you have treated yourself to chocolate every period for years, your brain has learned to expect it. That expectation is a craving too.

None of this is a willpower problem. It is a predictable pattern, and predictable patterns are easier to plan for.

Sugar cravings during your period

For many people, cravings peak in the last few days before bleeding starts and then calm down. Others notice them most on the first or second day, especially if they have cramps, low energy or poor sleep. Both are normal.

If you feel very tired during your period, it can be worth mentioning to your doctor, especially if your periods are heavy. Heavy bleeding can lead to low iron, and low iron can make you feel exhausted.

9 things that help with period sugar cravings

1. Mark the days on your calendar

Most cravings follow your cycle. If you track your period, note the days your cravings start. Next month you will know when they are coming, and they will feel less like a sudden loss of control.

2. Eat regular meals with protein and fiber

Skipping meals makes cravings louder. In the week before your period, keep regular meals and add protein (eggs, beans, yogurt, chicken, tofu) and fiber (vegetables, whole grains, fruit). A steady meal takes a lot of the edge off a 4 pm craving.

3. Plan a sweet you actually enjoy

Banning sweets completely often backfires and leads to eating far more later. Instead, decide on something you love and enjoy it on purpose: a couple of squares of dark chocolate after dinner, a small bowl of ice cream, a warm cookie. Eating it slowly, sitting down, usually feels more satisfying than grazing.

4. Keep easy swaps within reach

When the craving is strong and you want something quickly, have options ready:

  • Frozen grapes or frozen mango
  • Greek yogurt with berries and cinnamon
  • Dates with peanut butter
  • Apple slices with nut butter
  • A mug of hot cocoa made with milk and less sugar

5. Watch the sweet drinks

Soda, sweet coffee drinks and juice add up fast and do not fill you up. A single 12 oz can of regular cola has about 39 grams of sugar, more than the American Heart Association suggests for a whole day for most women (about 25 grams). Swapping the sweet drink is often the easiest win.

6. Protect your sleep

PMS can make sleep harder, and tired brains want sugar. A regular bedtime, a dark cool room and less screen time before bed can all help. If cravings hit late in the evening, our guide to night sugar cravings has more ideas.

7. Move a little

You do not need a hard workout. A 10 to 20 minute walk can lift your mood, ease cramps for some people and give a craving time to pass.

8. Use a warm drink as a pause

Herbal tea, warm milk or a decaf coffee gives your hands and mouth something to do. Many cravings fade within 15 to 20 minutes if you do something else first.

9. Drop the guilt

Eating a brownie before your period is not a failure. Feeling guilty is more likely to lead to "I already ruined it" eating than the brownie itself. Notice it, enjoy it, and get back to your normal meals next time you eat.

What about supplements?

You may see claims that magnesium, chromium or other supplements stop period cravings. The evidence is limited and mixed, and supplements can interact with medicines. If you are curious, ask your doctor or pharmacist before starting anything.

When to talk to a doctor

Talk to a doctor if your premenstrual symptoms are severe enough to disrupt work, school or relationships. Very strong mood changes before your period can be a sign of premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD), which is treatable. Also see a doctor if your periods are very heavy or painful, if you feel exhausted all the time, if you have diabetes, or if cravings come with episodes of eating large amounts while feeling out of control.

The short version

  • Craving sugar before or during your period is common and normal.
  • It is probably a mix of hormone shifts, a slightly bigger appetite, mood, sleep and habit.
  • Regular meals with protein and fiber, a planned treat, ready swaps and better sleep help most.
  • Be kind to yourself. A predictable craving is a pattern to plan for, not a failure.

Quick answers

Why do I crave sugar before my period?

In the week or so before a period, hormone levels shift, appetite often rises a little, and mood, sleep and energy can dip. Food cravings, often for sweet foods, are a common premenstrual symptom listed by groups like ACOG. The exact reasons are still being studied.

Is it normal to crave sugar during your period?

Yes. Many people notice stronger cravings in the days before their period and in the first day or two of bleeding. It usually eases once the period gets going.

What should I eat when I crave sugar on my period?

Regular meals with protein and fiber help most. For the craving itself, try fruit with yogurt, a couple of squares of dark chocolate, dates with nuts or a warm drink. You do not have to avoid sweet foods completely.

When should I see a doctor about period cravings?

If cravings come with mood changes that disrupt work or relationships, very heavy or painful periods, binge eating episodes, or if you have diabetes, talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian.
