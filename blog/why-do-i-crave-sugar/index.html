Why Do I Crave Sugar? 7 Common Causes and What to Do

The real reasons behind sugar cravings, from short sleep and stress to habit and long gaps between meals, and what to do about each one.

By the Pipgrove team. November 7, 2026, 3 min read.

Sugar cravings rarely come from nowhere. Most have a cause you can spot, and once you know which one is driving yours, they become much easier to handle. Here are the seven most common reasons, and what to do about each.

1. You are short on sleep

This is the most underrated cause. After a night of poor sleep, the brain's reward regions respond more strongly to high-calorie food while the areas that weigh decisions are quieter. In a UC Berkeley study, sleep-deprived people wanted more high-calorie foods than they did after a full night (Greer, Goldstein and Walker, Nature Communications, 2013).

What to do: treat sleep as part of your plan. A consistent bedtime does more for afternoon cravings than any snack trick.

2. Stress and big feelings

Sweet food is quick comfort. When you are stressed, sad, lonely or anxious, the brain reaches for the fastest reward it knows.

What to do: notice the feeling before the food. Ask "what am I actually feeling?" and pick a non-food comfort for that feeling: a walk, a call to a friend, a shower, five minutes outside.

3. Habit and timing

If you have had dessert after dinner every night for years, your brain starts asking for sugar at 8 pm whether you are hungry or not. The cue is the time and place, not hunger.

What to do: change the routine around the cue. Make tea after dinner, go for a short walk, or brush your teeth early. Plan an if-then rule: "If it is after dinner and I want something sweet, then I will have a cup of tea first."

4. Long gaps between meals

Skipping breakfast or lunch, or eating meals that are mostly refined carbs, sets up a steep dip in energy a few hours later. Sugar looks like the quickest fix.

What to do: eat regular meals built around protein, fibre and some fat. A mid-afternoon snack like yogurt with fruit or a handful of nuts can stop the slump before it starts.

5. Your taste is tuned to sweet

The more sweet food and drink you have, the more "normal" that level of sweetness feels. Hidden sugar in sauces, cereals, flavoured yogurt and drinks keeps the dial turned up.

What to do: cut back on added sugar for a few weeks and your taste starts to reset. Many people find fruit tastes noticeably sweeter after cutting back. Check labels for added sugars: 4 g is about one sugar cube.

6. Boredom

Sweet snacks are entertainment. Long afternoons at a desk or evenings in front of a screen invite grazing.

What to do: give your hands something else to do. A short walk, a glass of water, a puzzle, a few minutes of stretching. Cravings that come from boredom fade fast once you are doing something.

7. You are actually hungry

Sometimes a craving is simply hunger in disguise, especially if you have been dieting hard.

What to do: ask yourself whether an apple or a plate of eggs would sound good. If yes, you are hungry: eat a proper meal. If only cake will do, it is probably a craving, and the tips above apply.

What about deficiencies?

You will see claims online that sugar cravings mean you lack magnesium, chromium or another nutrient. The evidence for this in healthy people is weak. Sleep, stress, habit and meal timing explain most cravings. If you are worried about a deficiency, ask your doctor for a blood test rather than buying supplements on a hunch.

Why cravings are worse at night

Night-time is when several causes stack up: you are tired, the day's stress has built up, willpower is lower, and the after-dinner dessert habit kicks in. If your cravings cluster in the evening, focus on the routine after dinner and on getting to bed earlier.

When to talk to a doctor

If you have diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar, feel shaky or faint, or have a history of disordered eating, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian. Our health note lists support lines.

Quick answers

What deficiency causes sugar cravings?

There is no strong evidence that a single vitamin or mineral deficiency causes sugar cravings in healthy people. Sleep, stress, habit and irregular meals explain far more cravings. If you suspect a deficiency, ask your doctor for a blood test rather than guessing with supplements.

Why do I crave sugar at night?

Evenings stack up tiredness, stress, boredom and the habit of dessert after dinner. Willpower is lower when you are tired, and the brain learns to expect sugar at the same time each day.

Do sugar cravings go away?

For most people they get weaker and less frequent within a few weeks of cutting back, especially once the daily habits that trigger them change.
