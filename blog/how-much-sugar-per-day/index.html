How Much Sugar Per Day Is Too Much? (Free Calculator)

Daily added sugar limits for women, men and kids, what they look like in real food, and a quick calculator to check your own day.

By the Pipgrove team. November 11, 2026, 3 min read.

Most people have no idea how much added sugar they eat in a day, and that is not a character flaw. Sugar hides in drinks, sauces, breakfast foods and "healthy" snacks. This guide gives you the numbers, shows what they look like on a plate, and lets you check your own day in under a minute.

The short answer

Who Suggested limit for added sugar In teaspoons
Most women about 25 g a day about 6
Most men about 36 g a day about 9
Children 2 to 18 under 25 g a day under 6

These numbers come from the American Heart Association (AHA guidance on added sugars). The World Health Organization puts it another way: keep free sugars under 10% of your daily calories, and under 5% for extra benefit (WHO guideline). On a 2,000 calorie day, 10% is about 50 g and 5% is about 25 g.

Check your own day

Tap the things you had on a normal day. The cubes show your total, and the red ones go past your limit.

What counts as added sugar

Added sugar is any sugar or syrup put into food during cooking or processing, or added at the table. On a label it can be called sugar, cane sugar, corn syrup, high fructose corn syrup, honey, maple syrup, agave, dextrose, maltose, fruit juice concentrate and dozens more.

What does not count: the sugar that is naturally in whole fruit, vegetables, plain milk and plain yogurt. That is why the US Nutrition Facts label now lists Added Sugars on its own line. That line is the one to read.

Where the sugar actually comes from

For most people, a few sources make up most of the total:

  • Sweet drinks. One 12 oz can of regular soda has about 39 g, which is already over the daily limit for most women. See how much sugar is in a Coke.
  • Coffee shop drinks. Flavored lattes and iced coffees often carry 25 to 50 g.
  • Breakfast. Sweet cereal, flavored yogurt, granola and pastries add up before 9 am.
  • Snacks and desserts. Cookies, candy, ice cream and bars.
  • Sauces. Ketchup, barbecue sauce and many salad dressings carry a few grams per spoon.

How to get under the limit without misery

  1. Start with your biggest item. If the calculator shows one soda a day, swapping that one drink does more than cutting ten small things.
  2. Swap, do not just remove. Sparkling water with lemon, unsweetened iced tea, fruit with yogurt, or a few dates with nuts.
  3. Read one label a day. Look at Added Sugars. Divide by 4 to see the number of cubes.
  4. Expect cravings for a week or two. They usually get weaker as your taste resets. Our guide to sugar withdrawal explains what to expect.
  5. Plan for your craving time. Most cravings hit at the same time each day. Here is how to stop sugar cravings in the moment.

A note on health conditions

If you have diabetes, take medicine that affects blood sugar, are pregnant or have a history of disordered eating, ask your doctor or a registered dietitian what is right for you. Our health note lists support lines.

Quick answers

How many grams of sugar per day is OK?

The American Heart Association suggests no more than about 25 g of added sugar a day for most women and children and about 36 g for most men. The WHO suggests keeping free sugars under 10% of daily calories, and ideally under 5%.

Does the limit include sugar in fruit?

No. These limits are for added sugars, the ones put into food and drinks. Sugar that comes naturally in whole fruit, plain milk or plain yogurt is not counted.

How many teaspoons is 25 grams of sugar?

About 6 teaspoons. One teaspoon or one sugar cube holds roughly 4 g of sugar.

Is 50 grams of sugar a day too much?

For most adults, 50 g of added sugar is above the American Heart Association suggestion and around the WHO's 10% upper limit for a 2,000 calorie diet.
