How Much Sugar Is in Gatorade? Every Bottle Size, in Grams and Cubes

How much sugar is in Gatorade by bottle size, how it compares with soda and the daily limit, what is in Gatorade Zero, and when a sports drink actually makes sense.

By the Pipgrove team. November 27, 2026, 5 min read.

Gatorade sits in a strange spot. It is sold next to water, it is linked with athletes, and it says "thirst quencher" on the label, so many people do not think of it as a sugary drink. When you start paying attention to sugar, it is one of the bottles that surprises people most.

The short answer: a 20 oz bottle of Gatorade Thirst Quencher has about 34 grams of sugar. That is around 8 to 9 sugar cubes, and more than the 25 grams a day the American Heart Association suggests for most women.

How much sugar is in Gatorade? The numbers by size

The US label for the classic Gatorade Thirst Quencher lists 34 g of sugar in a 20 fl oz bottle. Other sizes scale from that.

Size Sugar Cubes (about 4 g each)
12 oz (355 ml) ~21 g ~5
20 oz bottle (591 ml) 34 g ~8 to 9
28 oz bottle ~48 g ~12
32 oz bottle ~54 g ~13 to 14
Gatorade Zero, 20 oz 0 g 0

Flavors are very close to each other, so the flavor you pick matters much less than the size. The bigger 28 and 32 oz bottles are where the grams add up fast, because they look like one drink but hold one and a half to almost two servings of the 20 oz bottle.

What kind of sugar is in Gatorade?

The classic formula is sweetened mainly with sucrose (table sugar) and dextrose (a form of glucose). That is on purpose. During long exercise, muscles use glucose as fuel, and a drink with a moderate amount of sugar plus salt is absorbed quickly. Gatorade was built for that situation.

Gatorade's sugar concentration is lower than soda. Per ounce, it has a little over half the sugar of Coca-Cola. That is why the 20 oz bottle lands near a 12 oz can of Coke rather than far above it. If you want to compare with soda directly, our post on how much sugar is in Coke has the cans and bottles side by side.

Gatorade and your daily sugar limit

The sugar in Gatorade is added sugar, the kind that daily limits are about. The American Heart Association suggests at most about 25 g a day for most women and 36 g for most men. The World Health Organization suggests keeping free sugars under 10% of daily calories, and ideally closer to 5%.

So:

  • One 20 oz bottle (34 g) uses up a full day's suggested amount for most women and almost all of it for most men.
  • A 32 oz bottle (~54 g) goes past both.
  • A 12 oz serving (~21 g) is most of a day's suggested amount for most women.

You can see your own number with the calculator in how much sugar per day.

None of this means one bottle is a problem. It just helps to know that a sports drink "counts" the same way a soda does.

When a sports drink makes sense

Sports drinks are not useless. They have a real job:

  • Long exercise. For activity that runs longer than about an hour, such as a long run, a ride, a full game or a hot day of physical work, the sugar can help with energy and the sodium helps replace what you lose in sweat.
  • Heavy sweating in the heat. Salt matters more when you sweat a lot.
  • Getting fluids in when you are sick. Some people find flavored drinks easier to sip. For real dehydration, oral rehydration solutions are made for that job and are worth asking a pharmacist about.

For most everyday situations, like a 30 to 45 minute gym session, a walk, at your desk or with lunch, water does the job and you skip the sugar. The American Academy of Pediatrics has said much the same about children: for most kids playing sports, water is the right drink, and sports drinks are for prolonged, intense activity.

Lower-sugar options

If you like the taste or the habit of a sports drink, a few swaps keep the feel without most of the sugar:

  • Gatorade Zero. 0 g sugar, sweetened with sucralose and acesulfame potassium, still has electrolytes. A good bridge if the bottle is a habit.
  • Lower-sugar versions such as G2 have less sugar than the classic formula. Check the label for the exact grams, since they change between products and countries.
  • Half and half. Fill half the bottle with Gatorade and half with water. Same flavor, half the sugar. Many people stop noticing the difference within a couple of weeks.
  • Water with a pinch of flavor. Sparkling water, a slice of citrus, or cold water in a bottle you like.
  • Electrolyte tablets or powders without sugar for long sweaty days.

A quick label check

When you pick up any sports or "hydration" drink, look at:

  1. Servings per container. Some bottles list more than one serving.
  2. Added sugars. On US labels, this line shows exactly how much sugar was added.
  3. Sodium. If you are not sweating a lot, you probably do not need the extra salt either.

The same habit works for juice, energy drinks and iced coffee. If energy drinks are part of your day, see how much sugar is in Red Bull.

When to talk to a doctor

If you have diabetes or prediabetes, kidney disease, high blood pressure, or you are using sports drinks to manage frequent dizziness, cramps or dehydration, talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian about what to drink. If you use sports drinks to treat low blood sugar, follow the plan your care team gave you.

  • A 12 oz serving has about 21 g; a 32 oz bottle about 54 g.
  • Per ounce, Gatorade has a bit over half the sugar of Coke.
  • Gatorade Zero has no sugar.
  • For workouts under about an hour, water is usually enough.

Quick answers

How much sugar is in a 20 oz Gatorade?

A 20 fl oz bottle of Gatorade Thirst Quencher has about 34 grams of sugar, roughly 8 to 9 sugar cubes, according to the US label. A 12 oz serving has about 21 grams.

Does Gatorade have more sugar than Coke?

Ounce for ounce, no. Gatorade has a little over half the sugar of Coca-Cola. But a 20 oz Gatorade bottle (34 g) still has almost as much sugar as a 12 oz can of Coke (39 g), and many people drink the whole bottle.

Does Gatorade Zero have sugar?

Gatorade Zero has 0 grams of sugar. It is sweetened with sucralose and acesulfame potassium and still contains the electrolytes sodium and potassium.

Do I need Gatorade when I work out?

For most workouts under about an hour, water is enough. Sports drinks were designed for long or very sweaty exercise, where the sugar and salt help. For everyday drinking they mostly add sugar.
