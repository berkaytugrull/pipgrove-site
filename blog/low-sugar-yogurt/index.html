Low Sugar Yogurt: How Much Sugar Is in Yogurt and How to Pick One

How much sugar is in yogurt, from plain and Greek to fruit-flavored cups, in grams and sugar cubes. How to read the label, what counts as low sugar yogurt, and easy ways to sweeten plain yogurt.

By the Pipgrove team. November 23, 2026, 5 min read.

Yogurt has a healthy reputation, and plain yogurt mostly earns it. But the yogurt aisle is also one of the easiest places to pick up a surprising amount of sugar without noticing. Two cups can look almost the same and differ by 15 grams of sugar.

Here is the short answer: plain yogurt has about 4 to 12 grams of sugar per cup, all of it lactose from milk. Flavored yogurt often has 12 to 25 grams, and a good part of that is added. Below are the numbers, how to read the label in a few seconds, and simple ways to make plain yogurt taste like a treat.

How much sugar is in yogurt?

Approximate sugar per serving, based on USDA FoodData Central for plain yogurts and typical US labels for the rest:

Yogurt Sugar Added Cubes
Plain whole-milk, 6 oz ~8 g 0 g ~2
Plain low-fat, 6 oz ~12 g 0 g ~3
Plain nonfat Greek, 6 oz ~5 g 0 g ~1
Plain skyr, 5.3 oz ~4 g 0 g ~1
Fruit-flavored, 6 oz ~15-25 g ~8-15 g ~4-6
Flavored Greek, 5.3 oz ~10-16 g ~5-10 g ~3-4
Unsweetened plant yogurt ~0-2 g 0 g 0

Brands vary a lot, so treat this as a map, not a rulebook. One sugar cube is about 4 grams.

Why plain yogurt still shows sugar

Yogurt starts as milk, and milk naturally carries lactose (a cup of milk has about 12 grams, see how much sugar is in milk). The live cultures that turn milk into yogurt use up part of that lactose, but some remains, so even unsweetened yogurt shows a few grams of sugar on the label.

That is why the total sugars number on its own can be misleading. A plain low-fat yogurt with 12 grams of lactose and a flavored yogurt with 12 grams of mostly added sugar look the same on that line. The line that tells them apart is the one underneath.

How to find a low sugar yogurt on the label

Since 2020, US Nutrition Facts labels show "Includes Xg Added Sugars" under total sugars. That one line does most of the work:

  1. Look at added sugars first. Plain yogurt reads 0 g. Many flavored cups read 8 to 15 g.
  2. Then glance at the ingredients. Cane sugar, honey, agave, fruit juice concentrate and syrups all count as added sugar. Fruit preparations often include sugar too.
  3. Compare per 100 g, not per cup. Cup sizes range from 4 to 8 oz, so a "lower sugar" cup can just be a smaller one.

There is no official US definition of "low sugar." In the UK and EU, a product can only say "low sugar" when it has 5 g of sugar or less per 100 g. A 2018 study in BMJ Open that checked more than 900 yogurts in UK supermarkets found that only a small minority met that threshold, and many organic and children's yogurts were among the higher-sugar products. A useful everyday rule: aim for 0 g added sugar, or only a few grams.

How much added sugar fits in a day?

The American Heart Association suggests keeping added sugar to about 25 g a day for most women and 36 g for most men. The World Health Organization recommends keeping free sugars under 10% of daily energy, and ideally under 5%. Neither counts the lactose in plain yogurt.

That puts a 12 g added sugar yogurt at about half of a 25 g day before lunch. It is not a reason to panic, but it is a good reason to look at the label. For the bigger picture, see added sugar vs. natural sugar and how much sugar per day.

8 easy ways to sweeten plain yogurt

Plain yogurt can taste sharp at first. These make the switch easier:

  1. Fresh or frozen berries. Thawed frozen berries release juice that colors and sweetens the whole bowl.
  2. Mashed ripe banana. Half a banana stirred in tastes like a banana pudding.
  3. Cinnamon and vanilla extract. Both make yogurt taste sweeter without adding sugar.
  4. Grated apple or pear. Add a pinch of cinnamon for an apple pie feel.
  5. Unsweetened cocoa plus banana. A quick chocolate pudding.
  6. Chopped dates or a few raisins. Their sugar still counts, so a small spoonful is plenty.
  7. Nuts, seeds or a little peanut butter. Fat and crunch make the bowl more filling and more satisfying.
  8. Mix half and half. If you love your flavored yogurt, mix it with plain for a week or two, then shift the ratio. Your taste adjusts more than you might expect.

Is Greek yogurt or skyr better?

Plain Greek yogurt and skyr are strained, so they are thicker, higher in protein and a little lower in lactose than regular plain yogurt. That extra protein can help a breakfast or snack hold you longer. But "Greek" on the label does not mean low sugar. Flavored Greek cups often carry 5 to 10 g of added sugar, so the same label check applies.

What about plant-based yogurt?

Unsweetened almond, coconut, soy or oat yogurts can have close to 0 g of sugar. "Original" or flavored versions often have added sugar, sometimes as much as dairy flavored cups. If you pick a plant yogurt for its protein, soy-based ones tend to have the most; many coconut and almond yogurts are low in protein.

When to talk to a doctor

If you have diabetes, ask your doctor or a dietitian how yogurt fits your carbohydrate plan, since lactose raises blood sugar like any carbohydrate. Talk to a doctor too if dairy regularly gives you bloating, cramps or diarrhea, or if you are choosing yogurt for a baby or young child. This article is general information, not medical advice.

The short version

  • Plain yogurt has about 4 to 12 g of sugar per cup, all lactose, and 0 g of added sugar.
  • Flavored yogurt often has 12 to 25 g, with 8 to 15 g added.
  • Read the "Added Sugars" line, and compare per 100 g.
  • Buy plain and sweeten it yourself with fruit, cinnamon or a half-and-half mix.

Quick answers

How much sugar is in yogurt?

Plain yogurt has roughly 4 to 12 grams of sugar per 6 oz cup, and all of it is lactose from the milk. Fruit-flavored and vanilla yogurts usually have about 12 to 25 grams, because sugar or fruit preparations are added on top of the lactose.

What is a low sugar yogurt?

There is no official US definition. A practical rule is a yogurt with 0 grams of added sugar on the label, or only a few grams. In the UK and EU, a "low sugar" claim is allowed only when a food has 5 grams of sugar or less per 100 grams.

Does Greek yogurt have less sugar?

Plain Greek yogurt usually does. Straining removes part of the liquid whey, and some of the lactose goes with it, so plain nonfat Greek yogurt often has around 5 to 6 grams of sugar per 6 oz, with more protein. Flavored Greek yogurt can have as much sugar as any other flavored yogurt.

Is the sugar in plain yogurt bad for you?

The lactose in plain yogurt is not added sugar under FDA labeling, and the World Health Organization does not count milk sugars as free sugars. It does not count toward the daily added sugar limits. The sugar to watch is the added sugar in flavored cups.
